# Icon style

## Voice

Construction paper, kid scissors and a glue stick. Solid yellow shapes with slightly snipped, faceted edges, a thin blue crease where paper folds, and a pop of bubblegum pink for the one special bit. Crafty and tactile rather than drawn; the craft-table cousin of the crayon.

## Drawing rules

1. Objects are solid plane silhouettes with no outline; line is only for folds, creases and inner cuts.
2. Curves are built from short straight snips of 3–5 units, so circles read as gently faceted polygons.
3. Corners are near-sharp (`box` or `snip`); softness comes from the facets, not from radius.
4. Layered parts sit on top of each other like glued paper, the upper layer offset 1 unit to show the one below.
5. Pink accent marks one part per icon: the eraser body, the sticker, the magnet, the smoke.
6. Line strokes are 1.5 with butt ends and never form closed outlines.
7. No shape narrower than 2 units, since that could not be cut by a kid.

## Vocabulary

- Paper house with a pink cut-paper smoke puff: home.
- Thick yellow crayon cut in three pieces, wrapper, body and tip: new-sketch.
- Scrapbook with a pink doodle page glued half out: my-drawings.
- Open picture book folded down the middle with a blue crease: storybook.
- Sheet with a star where only the left half is yellow paper: coloring-pages.
- Crayon box with a folded-back lid flap: crayon-box.
- Pink star sticker with one folded corner showing the crease: stickers.
- Pink block eraser with a yellow paper sleeve: eraser.
- Fridge door panel with a pink round magnet on a yellow drawing: fridge-gallery.
- Padlock silhouette with a keyhole cut through it and a key beside it: grown-ups.

## Never

- Closed outlines around objects; paper cutouts have edges, not strokes.
- Perfectly smooth vector curves; they break the scissors illusion.
- Gradients, shadows or paper-grain textures.
- Pink on more than one part, or pink used as the main body colour except for eraser and stickers.
- Rounded caps or joins.
